What Happens If You Ignore an HOA Violation? (Real Consequences)

DisputeMyHOA Team

January 2026 • 10 min read

You've got that HOA violation letter sitting on your kitchen counter. Maybe you're hoping it will go away. Maybe you're frustrated and want to make a point. Maybe you're just too busy to deal with it right now.

We get it. The temptation to ignore it is real. But before you toss that letter in a drawer, you need to understand exactly what happens next—because the consequences of ignoring an HOA violation are far more serious (and expensive) than most homeowners realize.

The Real Timeline of Ignoring an HOA Violation

Day 0: Initial Violation Notice

You receive a formal letter identifying the alleged violation, citing the relevant rule, and giving you a deadline to cure (fix) the issue or respond.

Days 15-30: Second Notice / Warning

A follow-up notice, often with stronger language. This may mention that fines will begin if the violation isn't addressed. This is your last chance before fines start.

Days 30-45: Fines Begin

Daily or weekly fines start accumulating. Common fine structures: $25-100 per day, $50-200 per week, or $100-500 per occurrence. A $50/day fine becomes $1,500 by the end of the first month.

Days 60-90: Lien Warning

A notice that the HOA intends to place a lien on your property for unpaid fines. A lien is a legal claim against your property that must be satisfied before you can sell or refinance.

Days 90-120: Lien Filed

The HOA files a lien with your county recorder's office. This lien is now part of your property's public record. You cannot sell without paying it off, cannot refinance, and it may affect your credit score.

Days 120+: Collections or Legal Action

The HOA may turn your account over to collections, file a lawsuit to collect the debt, or in extreme cases, pursue foreclosure. Total costs at this point: $7,500-25,000+.

Real Cost Breakdown

Consider this scenario: You receive a violation for an unapproved fence color. Cost to comply immediately: $200-500. But if you ignore it, by Day 180 you could face $11,700+ in fines, legal fees, and lien costs—plus you still have to repaint the fence.

How to Stop the Escalation at Any Stage

Before Fines Start (Best Option)

Respond in writing before the deadline. Acknowledge the notice and state your plan to comply (with timeline), or explain why you're disputing the violation. This often resolves the issue with no fines.

After Fines Have Started

Respond immediately and request a fine waiver or hearing. Explain circumstances, state your compliance plan, and request fines be waived or reduced given your good faith response.

After Lien Warning

Respond urgently and propose a resolution. Take the matter seriously, propose a payment plan if needed, and request a meeting or hearing.

Impact on Selling Your Home

Planning to sell eventually? Ignoring HOA violations can complicate or kill your sale. Liens must be satisfied at closing. Some buyers walk away when they see HOA problems. Lenders may refuse to finance homes with outstanding violations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will the HOA really sue me over a violation?

Yes, HOAs can and do sue homeowners over violations, especially when significant fines have accumulated. The question isn't whether they can sue—it's whether you want to spend $10,000+ defending yourself.

Can the HOA put a lien on my house?

Yes. In most states, HOAs have the statutory right to place liens on properties for unpaid assessments, fines, and related collection costs.

Can the HOA foreclose on my home for a violation?

In some states, yes. HOAs can foreclose on liens for unpaid fines, though this typically requires the debt to reach a significant threshold.
