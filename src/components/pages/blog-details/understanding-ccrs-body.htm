Understanding Your HOA CC&Rs: A Homeowner's Guide (2026)

DisputeMyHOA Team

January 2026 • 13 min read

If you own a home in a planned community, you've probably heard the term CC&Rs thrown around. Maybe you received a thick document at closing that you never quite got around to reading. Or perhaps you received a violation notice citing your CC&Rs and suddenly realized you need to understand what's in them.

Your HOA CC&Rs (Covenants, Conditions & Restrictions) are essentially the constitution of your homeowners association. They govern what you can and can't do with your property, establish your rights and responsibilities, and provide the framework for how your community operates.

What Are HOA CC&Rs?

CC&Rs stands for Covenants, Conditions & Restrictions. Let's break down what each term means:

Covenants

Promises or agreements that run with the land. When you buy a home in an HOA community, you automatically agree to abide by these covenants. Examples include: agreement to pay HOA dues, promise to maintain your property to certain standards, commitment to follow community rules.

Conditions

Requirements that must be met for certain rights to apply. Examples: approval required before making exterior modifications, compliance with rules required to use common facilities, good standing required for voting rights.

Restrictions

Limitations on what you can do with your property. Examples: prohibitions on certain paint colors, limits on the types of vehicles you can park, restrictions on renting your property.

The HOA Document Hierarchy

  1. Federal and State Laws (Highest Priority): All HOA documents must comply with applicable laws
  2. CC&Rs (Declaration): The primary governing document, most difficult to change
  3. Bylaws: How the association operates—elections, meetings, procedures
  4. Rules and Regulations: Board-adopted rules for specific issues
  5. Architectural Guidelines: Detailed standards for exterior modifications

Key Sections of CC&Rs to Understand

  • Property Descriptions and Definitions: Key terms used throughout the document
  • Use Restrictions: What you can and can't do with your property
  • Architectural Controls: Changes to your property's exterior
  • Assessment Obligations: Your financial obligations
  • Enforcement and Violations: How violations are handled
  • Amendment Procedures: How CC&Rs can be changed

CC&Rs and Your Rights

Rights You Typically Have

  • Use of common areas: Access to pools, clubhouses, parks
  • Due process: Notice and hearing before fines or enforcement
  • Voting: Voice in association decisions
  • Access to records: Right to review HOA documents and finances
  • Equal treatment: Rules must be applied consistently

When CC&Rs Can't Be Enforced

Not everything in your CC&Rs is enforceable. Provisions may be unenforceable if they: violate Fair Housing Laws, conflict with state laws, are unreasonable or arbitrary, have been abandoned, or are ambiguous.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can CC&Rs be changed without my consent?

Yes, if the required percentage of homeowners approves the change. You're bound by amended CC&Rs even if you voted against the change. However, some states require your individual consent for changes that specifically affect your property.

What's the difference between CC&Rs and HOA rules?

CC&Rs are recorded with the county and are difficult to change. Rules are adopted by the board and can be changed more easily. Rules must be consistent with CC&Rs—they can provide more detail but can't contradict the CC&Rs.

Can I be fined for violating CC&Rs?

Yes, if your CC&Rs authorize fines and proper procedures are followed. However, many states require that fine schedules be specifically adopted and that homeowners receive notice and an opportunity to be heard.
